Laura May-Lung Cha – Chairman of the International Advisory Council and Former Chairman of Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX)

---

### 1. Career

− Mrs Laura M. Cha currently serves as Chairman of the International Advisory Council and the Mainland China Advisory Group of Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX), advisory bodies she has chaired since stepping down as HKEX's Board Chairman in April 2024. She previously served a one-year term as Senior Advisor to the HKEX Board immediately following her chairmanship. Her HKEX chairmanship, which ran from 2018 to 2024, made her the first woman to hold that position in the exchange's history and encompassed a period of significant international expansion, including the opening of overseas offices and an unsuccessful attempt to acquire the London Stock Exchange Group.
  - o From April 2024 to present, Cha has served as Chairman of the International Advisory Council and the Mainland China Advisory Group of HKEX, bodies that advise the HKEX Board on international and mainland Chinese market development.
  - o From 25 April 2024 to 24 April 2025, Cha served as Senior Advisor to the HKEX Board, a one-year advisory appointment made upon her retirement from the Board following the 2024 AGM.
  - o From April 2018 to April 2024, Cha served as Chairman of Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited, the first woman to hold the role in the exchange's history; during her tenure HKEX pursued an unsolicited US$ 39 Bn takeover proposal for London Stock Exchange Group (rejected in September 2019 and withdrawn in October 2019), opened international offices in London, New York and Singapore, and advanced Stock Connect and listing-regime reforms including the April 2018 introduction of weighted-voting-rights listings.
  - o From 2013 to 2018, Cha served as Chairman of Hong Kong's Financial Services Development Council, a government advisory body on the development of the city's financial services industry.
  - o From 2007 to 2012, Cha served as Chairman of the University Grants Committee of Hong Kong, which oversees public funding for the territory's universities.
  - o From 2004 to 2022, Cha was a member of the Executive Council of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Government, the body that advises the Chief Executive on policy matters.
  - o From January 2001 to September 2004, Cha served as Vice Chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC), becoming the first, and to date only, person from outside mainland China to be appointed to the Central People's Government of the People's Republic of China at vice-ministerial rank.
  - o From 1991 to 2000, Cha worked at Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission (SFC), becoming its Deputy Chairman in 1998.
  - o From 1983 to 1991, Cha practised law in San Francisco and Hong Kong prior to entering securities regulation.

### About Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX)

− HKEX's identity and strategic positioning:
  - o Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX) is a publicly listed company that owns and operates Hong Kong's securities and derivatives markets and their related clearing houses, as well as the London Metal Exchange; it is not owned by, or affiliated with, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund.
  - o HKEX's stated strategy is to strengthen Hong Kong's role as an international connector between mainland China and global capital markets by expanding its multi-asset product suite, international office network, and underlying technology infrastructure.

### 2. Business Description

− HKEX's operations and revenue model:
  - o HKEX owns and operates Hong Kong's stock exchange, futures exchange, and securities and derivatives clearing houses, as well as the London Metal Exchange (LME), providing listing, trading, clearing and settlement, and market data services across equities, derivatives, commodities, and fixed income.
  - o Its most well-known platforms and franchises include The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK), Hong Kong Futures Exchange (HKFE), Hong Kong Securities Clearing Company (HKSCC), and the London Metal Exchange (LME), all wholly owned; it also operates the Stock Connect and Bond Connect mutual-market-access programs linking Hong Kong with mainland Chinese exchanges.
  - o HKEX reaches issuers, investors and intermediaries primarily through its exchange trading and clearing infrastructure, its network of international representative offices (including London, New York, Singapore, Beijing and Shanghai), and direct engagement with listing applicants and market participants.
  - o Its primary revenue drivers are trading and clearing fees generated from transaction volumes across its cash equities, derivatives and commodities markets, together with listing fees, market data fees and treasury income earned on margin and clearing collateral balances; trading and clearing fees rose materially in FY 2025 on record market turnover and IPO activity.

### 6. Significant Events

− Corporate history, in chronological order:
  - o 1891: The Association of Stockbrokers in Hong Kong, the forerunner of Hong Kong's stock exchange, was established.
  - o 1986: The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong Limited (SEHK) was formed through the merger of Hong Kong's four existing stock exchanges.
  - o 2000: Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited was incorporated on 6 March 2000 through the demutualization and merger of SEHK, the Hong Kong Futures Exchange, and the Hong Kong Securities Clearing Company into a single holding company.
  - o 2000: HKEX listed its own shares on the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong on 27 June 2000, becoming a listed company on the market it operates.
  - o 2012: HKEX completed its acquisition of the London Metal Exchange (LME) for approximately US$ 2.2 Bn, its first major overseas acquisition.
  - o 2014: The Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect program launched, providing mutual market access between Hong Kong and mainland Chinese equity markets.
  - o 2016: The Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect program launched, extending mutual market access to the Shenzhen exchange.
  - o 2018: HKEX introduced listing-regime reforms permitting weighted-voting-rights (dual-class share) structures and pre-revenue biotech companies to list on the Main Board.
  - o 2018: Laura M. Cha was appointed Chairman of HKEX, the first woman to hold the position.
  - o 2019: HKEX made an unsolicited approximately US$ 39 Bn takeover proposal for London Stock Exchange Group; the proposal was rejected by LSEG's board in September 2019 and withdrawn by HKEX in October 2019.
  - o 2021: Bond Connect Southbound trading launched, extending mutual bond-market access in the opposite direction.
  - o 2022: HKEX opened a New York representative office, expanding its international office network.
  - o 2023: HKEX opened a London representative office.
  - o 2024: Nicolas Aguzin's tenure as Chief Executive Officer ended; Bonnie Y. Chan was appointed CEO effective March 2024, the first woman to hold the role.
  - o 2024: Laura M. Cha stepped down as Chairman upon conclusion of the 2024 AGM; Carlson Tong was appointed Chairman effective April 2024.
  - o 2024: HKEX announced plans to open a representative office in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in 2025.
  - o 2025: HKEX reported record full-year results, with revenue and other income of HK$ 29,161 Mn and profit attributable to shareholders of HK$ 17,754 Mn, driven by record trading turnover and a resurgence in IPO activity that saw Hong Kong rank first globally by IPO funds raised.
  - o 2025: HKEX advanced construction of a next-generation clearing and settlement platform to support T+1 settlement and secured space for a planned permanent headquarters at One Exchange Square, Central.

### 8. Strategy and Future

− Strategic priorities:
  - o HKEX's stated strategic focus is to strengthen Hong Kong's position as a "superconnector" between mainland China and international capital markets by broadening its multi-asset product ecosystem — expanding equities, derivatives, commodities, fixed income, and index and fund-services offerings — while deepening two-way capital flows through Stock Connect and Bond Connect.
  - o The company's revenue model continues to be driven predominantly by trading and clearing fees tied to market turnover; HKEX has stated it is "energizing" IPO activity and diversifying product offerings to reduce dependence on transaction-volume cyclicality, with core trading and clearing revenue rising 31.8% in FY 2025 on record cash, derivatives and commodities volumes.
  - o HKEX is investing substantially in technology infrastructure, including a next-generation clearing and settlement platform intended to support a shift to T+1 settlement, alongside continued technology capital expenditure through 2025–26 aimed at platform resilience and future product expansion; it has also committed to a new permanent headquarters at One Exchange Square to consolidate its Hong Kong operations.

### 9. Controversy

− Notable controversies, in reverse-chronological order:
  - o In August 2025, the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (a HKEX subsidiary, acting in its regulatory capacity) publicly censured former directors of TOMO Holdings Limited for failing to cooperate with investigations by the Securities and Futures Commission and the Exchange — the first use of this specific disciplinary power, illustrating ongoing friction between HKEX's dual roles as both a listed commercial exchange operator and a frontline market regulator.
  - o In October 2019, HKEX withdrew its unsolicited approximately US$ 39 Bn takeover approach for London Stock Exchange Group, after LSEG's board rejected the proposal the previous month citing "fundamental" flaws; the bid had also faced a cool reception in mainland Chinese state media, which HKEX cited among its reasons for withdrawing the offer.
  - o In April 2018, HKEX's introduction of weighted-voting-rights (dual-class share) listings drew public criticism from governance advocates who argued the reform undermined the "one share, one vote" principle by privileging founder-shareholders over other investors.
